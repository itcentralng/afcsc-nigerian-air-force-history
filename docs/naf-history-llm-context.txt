NIGERIAN AIR FORCE HISTORY — REFERENCE CONTEXT

About this document: a factual reference on the history of the Nigerian Air Force (NAF), compiled for the Air Marshal S.K. Aneke Hall of Fame at the Armed Forces Command and Staff College (AFCSC), Jaji. It covers the founding and development of the NAF from 1961, every Commander and Chief of the Air Staff, the history of the AFCSC and its Department of Air Warfare, NAF bases and the aircraft the NAF has operated. Information is current as of November 2026. Biographies of the Chiefs of the Air Staff draw on the Nigerian Air Force (airforce.mil.ng) and Wikipedia. Where this document is silent on a point, treat that point as not covered rather than inferring it.

# Key facts

- Established: 18 April 1964. The Nigerian Air Force was formally established with the passage of the Air Force Act 1964 by the National Assembly.
- Leadership: 23 heads of service to date. The first two (Colonel Gerhard Kahtz and Colonel Wolfgang Thimmig) were German Air Force officers who served as Commanders of the NAF; from 19 January 1966 the service has been led by Nigerian officers.
- Serving Chief of the Air Staff: Air Marshal Sunday Kelvin Aneke, the twenty-third head of the service, in office since 30 October 2025. The Hall of Fame at AFCSC Jaji is named after him.
- Headquarters: Abuja (Headquarters Nigerian Air Force; Presidential Air Fleet).
- Commands: Tactical Air Command (Makurdi), Special Operations Command (Bauchi), Mobility Command (Yenagoa), Air Training Command (Kaduna), Ground Training Command (Enugu) and Logistics Command (Lagos).
- Birthplace of the NAF: Kaduna, where the first base was built from 1964.

# Timeline of the Nigerian Air Force, 1961 to 2026

## 1961–1971: Birth in a time of war

From the lessons of the Congo to a battle-tested force in under a decade: German instructors, the first Nigerian pilots, and the crucible of the civil war.

### 1961 — The idea of an air force is born
The idea of establishing an air force for Nigeria was first mooted in 1961 following the Nations participation in peace-keeping operations in Congo and Tanganyika (now Tanzania) where the Nigerian Army Regiment had no air transport of its own and were reliant on foreign air forces aircraft for airlift to and from the theatres of operation.

### 1962 — Government agrees to establish the NAF
The Nigerian Government agreed in principle the establishment of the Nigerian Air Force (NAF).

### June 1962 — First batch of cadets train in Ethiopia
First Batch of 10 cadets were enlisted to undergo training with the Imperial Ethiopian Air Force with only 4 successfully completing their training in 1964 and were enlisted as pioneer pilots of the Nigerian Air Force. These 4 pilots are: John Yisa-Doko (Northern Region), Daniel Suleiman (Northern region), Kola Falokpe (Western Region), and Onyerimba Faka (Eastern Region).

### February 1963 — Second batch of cadets train in Canada
A Second Batch of 16 cadets were enlisted to train with the Royal Canadian Air Force.

### 1962 — Third batch of cadets train in India
A Third Batch of 6 cadets were sent to train with the Indian Air Force.

### 1962 — West Germany wins the contract to build the Air Force
West Germany received the contract for the establishment of the Nigerian Air Force with technical assistance from the German Air Force Technical Assistance Group (GAFTAG). The head of GAFTAG was Colonel Gerhard Kahtz. He was later appointed as the first Commander of the Nigerian Air Force upon its formal establishment.

### 1962 — GAFTAG builds the Tactical and Training Wing
The Nigerian Air Force Tactical and Training Wing (NAFTTW) was built by the German Air Force Technical Assistance Group.

### 1962 — Fourth batch of cadets train in Germany
The Fourth Batch of cadets were sent to train with the German Air Force.

### 18 April 1964 — The Nigerian Air Force is formally established
The Nigerian Air Force was formally established with the passage of the Air Force Act 1964 by the National Assembly.

### 1964 — Ministry of Defence becomes NAF Headquarters
Ministry of Defence was the Headquarters of the Nigerian Air Force with Alhaji Muhammadu Ribadu as Minister of Defence (1960-1965).

### 1964 — Alhaji Tanko Galadima, first Minister of the Air Force
Alhaji Tanko Galadima was the First Minister of the Nigerian Air Force (1964-1966).

### 1964 — The first-generation aircraft arrive
The Nigerian Air Force commenced operations as an air transport and training unit. The Germans supplied the first-generation air assets which included the Aerospatiale Allouette helicopter, 20 Dornier DO-27 transport aircraft, 14 Piaggio 149D trainer aircraft, and Nord Noratlas platforms for transport.

### 1964 — The first four Army officers seconded to the NAF
The First 4 Nigerian Army officers seconded to the Nigerian Air Force were: Lieutenant Colonel George T Kurobo, Colonel Shittu Akanji Alao, Lieutenant Colonel Emmanuel E Ikwue, and Major John Esio Obada.

### 1964 — Construction of the first base begins at Kaduna
Construction of the first Nigerian Air Force base begun and was established in Kaduna.
Location: Kaduna State.

### 1964 — Malachy Njoku, the first Sergeant
Malachy Njoku was the First Sergeant of the Nigerian Air Force.

### 1964 — Squadron Leader John Chukwu, the first QFI
Squadron Leader John Chukwu was the First Qualified Flight Instructor (QFI) of the Nigerian Air Force.

### 1964 — The first Corporals
Lance Corporal Adamu and Lance Corporal Doyle were amongst the First Corporals of the Nigerian Air Force.

### 1964 — AD Bello, first NAF officer at the US Air War College
Air Vice Marshal AD Bello was the First NAF officer to attend the prestigious Air War College in the United States.

### 1964 — Light Liaison Transport Squadron established in Kaduna
Light Liaison Transport Squadron (Present day 401 Flying Training School) was established in Kaduna for converting NAF pilots trained overseas.
Location: Kaduna State.

### 24 November 1965 — Colonel W Timming appointed Second Commander
Colonel W Timming was appointed the Second Commander of the Nigerian Air Force.

### Jan 1966 — GAFTAG departs and hands over command
German Air Force Technical Assistance Group (GAFTAG) departed Nigeria and handed over command.

### 19 Jan 1966 — George T Kurubo, the first indigenous Commander
George T Kurubo was appointed the Third Commander and was the first indigenous Commander of the Nigerian Air Force. He held this office till 12 August 1967 when he was appointed Ambassador to the Soviet Union.

### Jan 1966 — The first NAF hangar commissioned at Kaduna
The First Nigerian Air Force hangar was commissioned at the Nigerian Air Force Base Kaduna.
Location: Kaduna State.

### 23 May 1966 — The first NAF plane crash, Ibadan
The First Nigerian Air Force plane crash occurred at Ibadan Airport killing Lieutenant Jasper Onyeji and Lieutenant D.F Onyirimba in a Piaggio P-149D while doing aerobatics that went sour.
Location: Oyo State.

### 6 July 1967 — The Nigerian Civil War begins
The Nigerian Civil War begins.

### 5 August 1967 — The first NAF helicopter crash
The First Nigerian Air Force helicopter crash occurred killing Colonel Joseph Akahan who was the Chief of Army Staff and two young pilots: 2nd Lieutenant G Ozie and 2nd Lieutenant Olawale Lawal. The aircraft crashed in Wannune village located between Gboko and Makurdi.
Location: Benue State.

### 5 August 1967 — Colonel Shittu Akanji Alao appointed Fourth Commander
Colonel Shittu Akanji Alao was appointed the Fourth Commander of the Nigerian Air Force. He held this office until his death on 15 October 1969 while on a solo flight in an L-29 aircraft in the Southern-western part of the country when he ran into bad weather and subsequently ran out of fuel. He tried to make an emergency landing, but instead, ran into a tree and died in the process. He died at Uzebba, about 50 miles Northwest of Benin.
He became the First Commander and Chief of the Air Staff to die on active duty.
Location: Edo State.

### 13 August 1967 — Biafran aircraft bomb Lagos
Biafran Aircraft bombs Lagos causing damages to the Nigerian Air Force fleet and installations.
Location: Lagos State.

### 1967 — First combat aircraft arrive from the Soviet Union
Acquisition of the First Batch of combat aircraft from the Soviet Union. The Nigerian Air Force received some unknown numbers of Mikoyan-Gurevich MiG-17Fs which were delivered to Kano from Egypt. Later, 6 Ilyushin Il-28 medium Bombers were delivered from Egypt and stationed at Calabar and Port Harcourt.
Location: Kano State.

### 1967 — Primary Flying Training Wing formed
The Light Liaison Transport Squadron was renamed Primary Flying Training Wing (PFTW) to undertake ab-initial flying training and earlier task of transport conversion.

### 5 June 1969 — MiG-17 downs a Red Cross DC-7
Nigerian Air Force MiG-17 allegedly operated by a British mercenary pilot guns down a Swedish Red Cross DC 7 plane while delivering relief supplies to Biafra. As a result, the Red Cross ceases air deliveries of aid.

### 17 December 1969 — Brigadier Emmanuel E Ikwue appointed Fifth Commander
Brigadier Emmanuel E Ikwue was appointed the Fifth Commander of the Nigerian Air Force and later became the First Nigerian Air Force Commander to hold the office now designated as the Office of the Chief of the Air Staff of the Nigerian Air Force. He held this Office till the 28 July 1975.

### 15 January 1970 — The Civil War ends
The Civil War ended. Below is an inventory of the total equipment damage inflicted on the Nigerian Armed Forces:

### 13 June 1970 — First Nigerian-trained cadet pilots graduate
The First Group of Nigerian trained Nigerian Air Force cadet pilots graduated in Kaduna.
Location: Kaduna State.

### 1971 — 7,000 personnel and 32 combat aircraft
Estimated figures showed the Nigerian Air Force owned about 7,000 personnel and 32 combat aircraft.

## 1972–1982: Building the force

A peacetime service takes shape. Hercules transports, MiG-21s and Alpha Jets arrive, and the institutions that still train officers today are founded.

### 29 July 1975 — AVM John Nmadu Yisa-Doko appointed Sixth Chief of the Air Staff
Air Vice Marshall John Nmadu Yisa-Doko was appointed the Sixth Chief of the Air Staff. He was the First Airman to be appointed Chief of the Air Staff. He is also the First to attain the rank of Air Vice Marshall in the Nigerian Air Force.

### 1975 — MiG-21 fighters purchased
25 Mikoyan-Gurevich MiG-21MFs and 6 MiG-21UM were purchased and delivered by the Murtala-Obasanjo administration.

### 1976 — The Air Force receives its Colours
The Nigerian Air Force was presented with its Colours by General Olusegun Obasanjo the Head of the Federal Military Government and Commander-in-Chief of the Armed Forces at the occasion of the First Air Force Day.

### 1 April 1976 — Full Air Force rank structure adopted
The Federal Military Government authorized the adoption of a complete Air Force rank structure to give the Nigerian Air Force full-service independence.

### 1977 — Training Supply School established in Kaduna
Nigerian Air Force Training supply school (TSS) was established in Kaduna as an indigenous technical supply school.
Location: Kaduna State.

### 1978 — Tactical Air Command and Training Command established
In a bid to restructure the Nigerian Air Force, the Tactical Air Command and the Training Command were established.

### 1979 — Technical Training Group, Kaduna
The Technical Supply School was renamed Technical Training Wing and redesignated NAF Technical Training Group (NAFTTG) Kaduna. It was responsible for providing basic training in aircraft maintenance, armament, communication and supply management.
Location: Kaduna State.

### 15 April 1980 — AVM Abdullahi Dominic Bello appointed Seventh Chief of the Air Staff
Air Vice Marshal Abdullahi Dominic Bello was appointed the Seventh Chief of the Air Staff. He held this Office till 31 December 1983.

### 1982 — Logistics Command established in Lagos
The Nigerian Air Force Logistics Command was established in Lagos.
Location: Lagos State.

## 1983–1993: Jaguars and expeditions

Modern strike jets enter service, and in 1990 the Air Force deploys abroad for the first time with ECOMOG in Liberia.

### 1983 — New wings for NAF aircrew
New wings for the Nigerian Air Force aircrew were introduced to replace the near foreign ones in use.

### 01 January 1984 — Air Marshal Ibrahim Mahmud Alfa appointed Eighth Chief of the Air Staff
Air Marshal Ibrahim Mahmud Alfa was appointed the Eight Chief of the Air Staff. He held this office until 01 January 1990.

### 1984 — 18 SEPECAT Jaguars delivered to Makurdi
18 SEPECAT Jaguar fighters were delivered and operated from Makurdi.
Location: Benue State.

### December 1986 — MB-339AN trainers at Calabar; L-39 Albatros purchased
The Tactical Air Command announced the establishment of a unit of Aermacchi MB-339AN trainers at Calabar.
24 Aero L-39 Albatros armed jet trainers were purchased.
Location: Cross River State.

### 1984 — L-39 replaces the L-29 Delfin
The L-39 were purchased after having retired its fleet of Aero L-29 Delfin.

### 1984 — PFTW becomes 301 Flying Training School
PFTW was upgraded to a group status and renamed 301 Flying Training School (301 FTS).

### March 1987 — Inter-command Communication System completed
The Nigerian Air Force completed the installation of the Inter-command Communication System.

### 1 October 1987 — Alfa becomes the first Air Marshal
Ibrahim Mahmud Alfa was the First Nigerian Air Force officer to be promoted to the rank of Air Marshal.

### June 1988 — Four specialised Commands
The Nigerian Air Force was organized into 4 specialized Commands which are: Tactical Command (Makurdi), Training Command (Kaduna), Logistics Command (Lagos), and Airlift Command (Ibadan).
Location: Kaduna State.

### 1989 — The Air Beetle: first locally built trainer
The Nigerian Air Force unveiled its first Locally built trainer aircraft dubbed the “Air Beetle”.

### 1990 — Air Marshal Nuraini Oladimeji Yussuff appointed Ninth Chief of the Air Staff
Air Marshal Nuraini Oladimeji Yussuff was appointed the Ninth Chief of the Air Staff. He held this Office until his retirement on 30 January 1992.

### 31 January 1992 — Air Marshal Akin Dada appointed Tenth Chief of the Air Staff
Air Marshal Akin Dada was appointed the Tenth Chief of the Air Staff. He held this Office until his retirement on 16 September 1993.

### 26 Sept 1992 — The NAF 911 Hercules tragedy, Ejigbo
A fatal air crash involving a Nigerian Air Force Lockheed C-130H Hercules 911, 3 minutes after take-off from Lagos when 3 engines failed killing all 158 passengers and crew members. It crashed at the Ejigbo Canal.
Location: Lagos State.

### 17 September 1993 — AVM Femi John Femi appointed Eleventh Chief of the Air Staff
Air Vice Marshal Femi John Femi was appointed the Eleventh Chief of the Air Staff. He held this Office until his retirement on 29 March 1996.

## 1994–2004: Peacekeeping and renewal

Operations over Sierra Leone, the return to democratic rule, and the long task of restoring a fleet worn down by sanctions.

### 30 March 1996 — Air Marshal Nsikak-Abasi Eduok appointed Twelfth Chief of the Air Staff
Air Marshal Nsikak-Abasi Essien Eduok was appointed as the Twelfth Chief of the Air Staff of the Nigerian Air Force. He held this Office until his retirement on 29 May 1999.

### 9 June 1998 — Al-Amin Daggash, first NAF Chief of Defence Staff
Air Marshal Al-Amin Daggash was the First Nigerian Air Force Chief of Defence Staff.

### 10 March 1999 — Habibah Ruth Garba, first female Air Commodore
Habibah Ruth Garba was the First female Air Commodore in the Nigerian Air Force.

### 29 May 1999 — Air Marshal Isaac Mohammed Alfa appointed Thirteenth Chief of the Air Staff
Air Marshal Isaac Mohammed Alfa was appointed as the Thirteenth Chief of the Air Staff of the Nigerian Air Force. He held this Office until his retirement on 23 April 2001.

### 2000 — 320 Technical Training Group, Kaduna
Technical Training Group was designated as 320 Technical Training Group (320 TTG), Kaduna.
Location: Kaduna State.

### 24 April 2001 — Air Marshal Jonah Domfa Wuyep appointed Fourteenth Chief of the Air Staff
Air Marshal Jonah Domfa Wuyep was appointed as the Fourteenth Chief of the Air Staff of the Nigerian Air Force. He held this Office until his retirement on 31 May 2006.

## 2005–2015: Rebuilding the fleet

New fighters from China, new partnerships, and the beginning of the counter-insurgency campaign in the North East.

### 2005 — $251 million approved for 15 Chengdu F-7 fighters
The Nigerian Government approved $251 million for the purchase of 15 Chengdu F-7 fighter jets from China. The package included $220 million for the 15 aircraft and $32 million for armaments.

### 01 June 2006 — Air Marshal Paul Dike appointed Fifteenth Chief of the Air Staff
Air Marshal Paul Dike was appointed as the Fifteenth Chief of the Air Staff of the Nigerian Air Force. He held this Office until his appointment as Chief of the Defence Staff on 19 August 2008.

### 17 Sept 2006 — Air crash at Obudu
Air crash involving several Army officers in Obudu, Cross-River State.
Location: Cross River State.

### 12 March 2008 — 320 TTG becomes the Air Force Institute of Technology
The 320 Technical Training Group on expansion and full accreditation by NBTE and NUC as well as its affiliation to Cranfield University was renamed Air Force Institute of Technology (AFIT).

### 12 March 2008 — AFIT is established
The Nigerian Air Force Institute of Technology (AFIT) was established.

### 20 August 2008 — Paul Dike, the first Air Chief Marshal
The First Nigerian Air Force officer to attain the rank of Air Chief Marshal was Air Marshal Paul Dike upon his appointment as the Chief of Defence Staff.

### 20 August 2008 — Air Marshal Oluseyi Petinrin appointed Sixteenth Chief of the Air Staff
Air Marshal Oluseyi Petinrin was appointed as the Sixteenth Chief of the Air Staff of the Nigerian Air Force. He held this Office until his appointment as Chief of the Defence Staff on 18 September 2010.

### 2008 — Pioneer F-7 pilots trained in China
The Nigerian Air Force trained the pioneer Chengdu F-7 fighter pilots in China while delivery of the aircraft began in 2009.

### September 2009 — C-130 refurbishment begins with NAF 917
The Nigerian Air Force began refurbishing some of its C-130 aircraft beginning with the NAF 917.

### 2010 — GULMA: the first indigenous UAV
The Nigerian Air Force designed and built its First indigenous Unmanned Aerial Vehicle (UAV) dubbed “GULMA” and was unveiled by former President Goodluck Jonathan in Kaduna.
Location: Kaduna State.

### 19 September 2010 — Air Marshal MD Umar appointed Seventeenth Chief of the Air Staff
Air Marshal MD Umar was appointed as the Seventeenth Chief of the Air Staff of the Nigerian Air Force. He held this Office until his retirement on 03 October 2012.

### 29 March 2011 — Mobility Command established in Yenagoa
The Nigerian Air Force established the Mobility Command with its Headquarters in Yenogoa.
Location: Bayelsa State.

### 9 December 2011 — Flt Lt Blessing Liman, the first female pilot
Blessing Liman, a graduate of the Nigerian College of Aviation Technology, enlisted in July 2011 and was commissioned on 9 December 2011 at 325 Ground Training Group, Kaduna, with 116 cadets of Direct Short Service Course 20/2011. On 27 April 2012 she received her wings from the Chief of the Air Staff, Air Marshal Mohammed Dikko Umar, becoming the first female pilot of the Nigerian Air Force.

### 04 October 2012 — Air Marshal Alex Sabundu Badeh appointed Eighteenth Chief of the Air Staff
Air Marshal Alex Sabundu Badeh was appointed the Eighteenth Chief of the Air Staff of the Nigerian Air Force. He held this Office until his appointment as the Fifteenth Chief of Defence Staff on 15 January 2014.

### 16 January 2014 — Air Marshal Adesola Nunayon Amosu appointed Nineteenth Chief of the Air Staff
Air Marshal Adesola Nunayon Amosu was appointed the Nineteenth Chief of the Air Staff of the Nigerian Air Force. He held this Office until his retirement on 12 July 2015.

### 18 April 2014 — Golden Jubilee: fifty years of the NAF
The Nigerian Air Force marked its 50 years Golden Jubilee Celebration.

### 25 September 2014 — CH-3A UCAV inducted
The CH-3A UCAV was inducted into the Nigerian Air Force fleet.

### 13 July 2015 — Air Marshal Sadique Baba Abubakar appointed Twentieth Chief of the Air Staff
Air Marshal Sadique Baba Abubakar was appointed the Twentieth Chief of the Air Staff of the Nigerian Air Force. He held this Office until his retirement on 25 January 2021.

## 2016–2026: A force reborn

Super Tucanos, JF-17 Thunders, sixty years of service and a new generation of leaders.

### 19 November 2016 — Special Operations Command established in Bauchi
The Nigerian Air Force established the Special Operations Command (SOC) in Bauchi.
Location: Bauchi State.

### 2016 — 301 FTS becomes 401 Flying Training School
301 FTS was redesignated 401 Flying Training School (401 FTS).

### 8 June 2017 — NAF Tailoring Workshop, Kaduna
The Nigerian Air Force established the Nigerian Air Force Tailoring Workshop in Kaduna.
Location: Kaduna State.

### 15 February 2018 — TSAIGUMI: first locally built operational UAV
The Nigerian Air Force inducted its first locally-built operational UAV drone dubbed the “TSAIGUMI”. The induction was done by the President and Commander-in-Chief of the Armed Forces, Muhammadu Buhari. The drone was made by the Nigerian Air Force Institute of Technology.

### 16 April 2018 — First set of Liberian pilots winged
Winging of the First Set of Liberian pilots that were trained for the Liberian Armed Forces.

### 2 May 2018 — Ground Training Command established in Enugu
The Nigerian Air Force established the Ground Training Command (GTC) in Enugu having been unbundled from the erstwhile Training Command on 4 July 2017.
Location: Enugu State.

### 28 September 2018 — F-7 collision over Abuja
A fatal air collision involving two F-7 jets as they practiced flight maneuvers for the 58th Independence Day Celebrations in Abuja. The 3 pilots ejected out of the crippled jets with 2 sustaining minor G-force injuries while the third pilot died on his way to the hospital after sustaining head injuries due to problems from the parachute as it deployed.
Location: FCT Abuja.

### November 2018 — Contract awarded for twelve A-29 Super Tucanos
Sierra Nevada was officially awarded the contract for twelve A-29 Super Tucano aircraft for the Nigerian Air Force with an estimated completion date by 2024.

### 2 January 2019 — Mi-35M crash at Damasak
A Mi-35M attack helicopter crashed in Damasak, Borno State while providing close-air support for troops of the 145 Battalion combating Boko Haram insurgents, killing all onboard.
Location: Borno State.

### 15 October 2019 — Lt Kafayat Sanni, first female fighter pilot
Kafayat Sanni enlisted on 22 December 2012 and, after emerging overall best pilot at 401 Flying Training School, Kaduna, in 2017, trained in the United States under the Aviation Leadership Programme. On 15 October 2019 in Abuja she was winged by Air Marshal Sadique Abubakar as the first female fighter pilot in the 55-year history of the Nigerian Air Force.

### 15 October 2019 — Lt Tolulope Arotile, first female combat helicopter pilot
Tolulope Oluwatoyin Sarah Arotile, born in Kaduna on 13 December 1995, was commissioned on 16 September 2017 from the NDA 64 Regular Course and trained on helicopters in South Africa. On 15 October 2019 she was winged as the first female combat helicopter pilot of the Nigerian Air Force, among thirteen pilots winged that day. She flew 460 hours in operations against the insurgency before her death in a road accident at NAF Base Kaduna on 14 July 2020.

### 15 October 2019 — Grace Garba, first female Air Warrant Officer
In October 2019 Master Warrant Officer Grace Garba became the first female Senior Non-Commissioned Officer to be promoted to the rank of Air Warrant Officer, the highest non-commissioned rank in the Nigerian Air Force.

### 28 May 2020 — First all-female ISR crew
The Nigerian Air Force recorded its First all-female crew to conduct an operational Intelligence, Surveillance and Reconnaissance (ISR) mission using the NAF Beechcraft King Air ISR aircraft.

### 2020 — Fg Offr Oluwabunmi Ijelu, first female Captain

### 26 January 2021 — Air Marshal Oladayo Isiaka Amao appointed Twenty-first Chief of the Air Staff
Air Marshal Oladayo Isiaka Amao was appointed the Twenty-first Chief of the Air Staff, an Office he holds till date.

An Alpha jet with pilot, Flight Lieutenant Chappelle and another crew member crashed near Borno State after conducting air interdiction on Boko Haram. Induction of three Pakistani JF-17 Thunder to the Nigerian Air Force. The Ceremony was done to mark the end of the 2021 Nigerian Air Force Day Celebration.

The crash occurred when the plane was trying to land in bad weather at the Kaduna International Airport. The crew members were Flt Lt TO Asaniyi, Flt Lt AA Olufade, Sgt Adesina and ACM Oyedepo.

### 18 July 2021 — Flt Lt Abayomi Dairo ejects and evades capture

Location: Kaduna State.

The First Batch of 6 A-29 Super Tucano aircraft arrived Kano, Nigeria. The aircraft were received by the Honourable Minister of Defence, Major General Bashir Magashi (Rtd), alongside the Chief of Army Staff, Lieutenant General Faruk Yahaya and the Chief of Air Staff, Air Marshal Oladayo Amao.

The newly acquired A-29 Super Tucano aircraft were inducted into the Nigerian Air Force (NAF) Order of Battle at the 011 Presidential Air Fleet Apron, Nnamdi Azikiwe International Airport, Abuja. The Commander-in-Chief of the Armed Forces of the Federal Republic of Nigeria, President Muhammadu Buhari, GCFR, presided over the Induction Ceremony as the Special Guest of Honour. The Super Tucanos received the following serials: NAF845, NAF846, NAF848, NAF850, NAF851 and NAF852.

# Commanders and Chiefs of the Air Staff: profiles

## 1. Colonel Gerhard Kahtz

- Position: Commander of the Nigerian Air Force (first head of the NAF)
- Rank: Colonel, German Air Force
- In office: 5 May 1963 to 11 November 1965 (2 years 6 months)
- Born: 1922

A Luftwaffe officer, Kahtz led the German Air Force Technical Assistance Group (GAFTAG), which received the contract to establish the Nigerian Air Force. He became the first Commander of the NAF, serving from 5 May 1963 to 11 November 1965.

Notable in office:
- Led GAFTAG, which built the NAF Tactical and Training Wing at Kaduna
- First-generation aircraft: Dornier Do-27, Piaggio P.149D, Nord Noratlas and Alouette
- Flying training began at Kaduna

## 2. Colonel Wolfgang Thimmig

- Position: Commander of the Nigerian Air Force (second head of the NAF)
- Rank: Colonel, German Air Force
- In office: 24 November 1965 to 19 January 1966 (56 days)
- Born: 1912
- Died: 1976

Appointed the Second Commander of the Nigerian Air Force on 24 November 1965, Thimmig held office for 56 days until GAFTAG handed over command to Nigerian officers on 19 January 1966.

Notable in office:
- Completed the handover of command to Nigerian officers (January 1966)

## 3. Lieutenant Colonel George Tamunoiyowuna Kurubo

- Position: Chief of the Air Staff (third head of the NAF)
- Rank: Lieutenant Colonel (later Brigadier)

# History of the College: Armed Forces Command and Staff College, Jaji

Established on 29 May 1976 with the help of the British Army Advisory Team, the College at Jaji trains tactical and operational level officers of the Army, Navy and Air Force in standardised command and staff duties.

### 29 May 1976 — The Army Command and Staff College is established
The Army Command and Staff College (ACSC) was established with assistance from the British Army Advisory Team (BAAT), led by Brigadier General T.A. Boam. General Olusegun Obasanjo, Head of State and Commander-in-Chief of the Armed Forces, delivered the inaugural speech. Brigadier General G.O. Ejiga was the pioneer Commandant.

### 29 May 1976 — Senior Officers Course 1
Inauguration of Senior Officers Course 1 (SOC 1), composed only of Army officers. The course lasted six months.

### January 1977 — A bi-service course
Inauguration of Senior Officers Course 2 (SOC 2), the first bi-service course, comprising Army officers and two Naval officers.

### 12 September 1977 — The College goes tri-service
Inauguration of Senior Officers Course 3 (SOC 3), a tri-service course of 62 Army officers, 2 Naval officers, 2 Air Force officers and 2 Army officers from Guyana.

### 24 April 1978 — First Junior Course of the Army Faculty
Inauguration of the first Junior Course of the Army Faculty.

### 1 September 1978 — ACSC becomes the Command and Staff College
The Army Command and Staff College was re-designated Command and Staff College (CSC), and CSC Course 1 was inaugurated.

### 26 February 1979 — Junior Course of the Air Faculty
Establishment of the Junior Course of the Air Faculty by Major General J.O. Bajowa, Commandant, Command and Staff College.

### 1 September 1981 — The Naval Faculty joins
Inauguration of CSC 4 with 13 students of the Naval Faculty.

### August 1984 — Tri-service insignia
The Junior Division of the Naval Faculty was established, and a new insignia was introduced to represent the tri-service status of the CSC.

### 1986 — A decade of graduates
By 1986, 1,172 officers had graduated from the Senior Divisions at Jaji and 1,320 from the Junior Divisions.

### 28 June 1985 — Danjuma Hall is commissioned
General Muhammadu Buhari, Head of State and Commander-in-Chief of the Armed Forces, at the commissioning of Danjuma Hall, CSC.

### October 1988 — The Joint Studies Division
The Joint Studies Division was established with the help of the British Joint Warfare Advisory Team (JWAT), whose members were hosted to a dinner before departing for the United Kingdom.

### 26 September 1992 — CSC 15 and the Ejigbo crash
Officers of CSC 15 were among those lost in the fatal C-130 Hercules crash at Ejigbo, Lagos State.

### 17 January 1999 — The first Air Force Commandant
Appointment of Air Vice Marshal A.O. Ogundana, the first Nigerian Air Force Commandant, after the leadership of the CSC was ceded to the NAF.

### 2 December 2002 — The Armed Forces Command and Staff College
The College was re-designated the Armed Forces Command and Staff College (AFCSC). The four Faculties became Departments: Joint Studies, Land Warfare, Air Warfare and Maritime Warfare. Principal Staff Officer appointments became Directors of Coordination, Administration, Logistics and Finance, and the Armed Forces Simulation Centre was established.

### 25 November 2012 — St Andrews Church, Jaji
A bomb blast at St Andrews Church in the Jaji Military Cantonment struck the College community.

### 2026 — The Hall of Fame opens
The Air Marshal S.K. Aneke Hall of Fame opens at the College, preserving the story of Nigerian airpower for every officer who passes through Jaji.

# History of the Department of Air Warfare, AFCSC Jaji

The Department of Air Warfare teaches airpower to every student of the College: how it is planned, commanded and integrated with land and maritime forces.

### 12 September 1977 — The first Air Force students
Two Nigerian Air Force officers joined Senior Officers Course 3, the first tri-service course of the College.

### 26 February 1979 — The Air Faculty
The Junior Course of the Air Faculty was established by Major General J.O. Bajowa, Commandant, Command and Staff College.

### August 1984 — Tri-service insignia
A new College insignia was introduced to represent the tri-service status of the CSC.

### 17 January 1999 — Air Force leadership of the College
Air Vice Marshal A.O. Ogundana was appointed the first Nigerian Air Force Commandant after the leadership of the CSC was ceded to the NAF.

### 2 December 2002 — The Department of Air Warfare
With the re-designation of the College as AFCSC, the four Faculties became Departments of Joint Studies, Land Warfare, Air Warfare and Maritime Warfare.

### The courses — Senior, Junior and Staff Duties
The Senior Divisions of the Land, Maritime and Air Warfare Departments run a one-year joint course for officers of the rank of Major or Squadron Leader; graduates are awarded the symbol psc. The Junior Divisions run two twenty-week courses a year for Captains and Flight Lieutenants, awarding pjsc. A Staff Duties Course serves Senior Non-Commissioned Officers of the three Services.

### 13 May 2026 — Senior Course 48 in the field
Participants of Senior Course 48 visited the Air Component of Operation Fansan Yamma Sector 2 in Katsina State to see NAF operational capabilities at first hand.

### 2026 — A home for the story
The Department becomes custodian of the Air Marshal S.K. Aneke Hall of Fame.

# Bases and installations

## Abuja (FCT Abuja)

Role: Headquarters Nigerian Air Force; Presidential Air Fleet.
Headquarters Nigerian Air Force sits in the Federal Capital Territory, having moved from Lagos after the seat of government relocated to Abuja. From here the Chief of the Air Staff directs the service and its Commands.
Abuja is also the home of the Presidential Air Fleet. On 31 August 2021 the first six A-29 Super Tucano aircraft were formally inducted into the NAF Order of Battle at the 011 Presidential Air Fleet Apron, Nnamdi Azikiwe International Airport, with President Muhammadu Buhari presiding.
The capital has seen the Air Force at its most visible: the Golden Jubilee celebrations of 2014, the induction of the CH-3A unmanned aircraft that same year, and the annual Independence Day flypasts rehearsed over the city.

## Kaduna (Kaduna State)

Role: Air Training Command; 401 Flying Training School; AFIT.
Kaduna is where the Nigerian Air Force was born. Construction of the first NAF base began here in 1964, the German Air Force Technical Assistance Group built the Tactical and Training Wing, and the Light Liaison Transport Squadron (today 401 Flying Training School) was established to convert pilots trained overseas.
The first NAF hangar was commissioned at Kaduna in January 1966, and in June 1970 the first group of Nigerian-trained cadet pilots graduated here. Sir Ahmadu Bello and Prime Minister Tafawa Balewa visited the training centre in 1965.
Technical training grew from the Training Supply School of 1977 into the Technical Training Group, then 320 TTG, and in 2008 the Air Force Institute of Technology (AFIT), which designed the GULMA and TSAIGUMI unmanned aircraft. Kaduna remains the home of Air Training Command.

## Jaji (Kaduna State)

Role: Armed Forces Command and Staff College.
Jaji, north of Kaduna, is home to the Armed Forces Command and Staff College, established in 1976 so that officers of the Army, Navy and Air Force could learn to plan and command together at the operational level.
The Department of Air Warfare teaches airpower doctrine, air campaign planning and joint operations to every student of the College. The Air Marshal S.K. Aneke Hall of Fame stands here.

## Makurdi (Benue State)

Role: Tactical Air Command; 101 Air Defence Group.
Makurdi became a key operating base during the civil war and has been the fighter heart of the Air Force ever since. Eighteen SEPECAT Jaguar strike fighters were delivered here in 1984, and in 1988 the Tactical Air Command was headquartered in the city.
The Chengdu F-7Ni fighters arrived from 2009, and on 20 May 2021 three JF-17 Thunder multirole fighters were inducted to mark the end of the NAF Day celebrations.
Benue is also the home state of Brigadier Emmanuel E. Ikwue, the fifth Commander and the first to hold the office of Chief of the Air Staff.
Headquarters Tactical Air Command directs all fighting units of the Air Force from Makurdi, alongside 101 Air Defence Group, 131 Engineering Group and the only NAF Central Armament Depot. The Air Force also operates its air-to-ground weapons range at Kwenev, near the city.

## Kano (Kano State)

Role: 403 Flying Training School.
Kano received the first MiG-17F fighters delivered from Egypt during the civil war in 1967, the first combat aircraft of the Nigerian Air Force.
On 22 July 2021 the first six A-29 Super Tucano aircraft landed at Kano after their ferry flight from the United States, received by the Minister of Defence, the Chief of Army Staff and the Chief of the Air Staff, Air Marshal Oladayo Amao.
Kano State is the home of Air Marshal Hasan Bala Abubakar, the twenty-second Chief of the Air Staff.
The first MiG-17s reached Kano via Egypt from 13 August 1967. Today Kano hosts 403 Flying Training School of the Air Training Command.

## Maiduguri (Borno State)

Role: 105 Composite Group; North East operations.
Maiduguri is the forward hub of the air campaign in the North East. From here Alpha Jets, Mi-35M helicopters, Super Tucanos and ISR aircraft have supported ground troops in Operations Lafiya Dole and Hadin Kai.
The campaign has cost lives: a Mi-35M attack helicopter was lost at Damasak on 2 January 2019 while providing close air support, and an Alpha Jet with Flight Lieutenant Chappelle and his crew went down near Borno on 31 March 2021.

## Yola (Adamawa State)

Role: 103 Strike Group.
Yola supports air operations across the North East, with helicopters and light attack aircraft operating from the Adamawa capital.
Adamawa is the home state of Air Marshal Alex Sabundu Badeh of Vimtim, the eighteenth Chief of the Air Staff, who became Chief of Defence Staff in January 2014.
103 Strike Group of the Tactical Air Command operates from Yola.

## Kainji (Niger State)

Role: 407 Air Combat Training Group.
Kainji has hosted Alpha Jet operations since the 1980s and is now the home of the A-29 Super Tucano fleet inducted in 2021.
Niger State is the home of Air Vice Marshal John Nmadu Yisa-Doko, one of the four pioneer pilots trained in Ethiopia, the first airman to become Chief of the Air Staff and the first to attain the rank of Air Vice Marshal.
Kainji is one of only four purely military airfields of the NAF, with Kaduna, Makurdi and Port Harcourt, and hosts 407 Air Combat Training Group and 41 Force Protection Wing.

## Minna (Niger State)

Role: NAF Detachment, Minna Airport.
The NAF Detachment at Minna operates from within Minna Airport, supporting air operations in the North Central region.
Niger State is also the home of Kainji and of Air Vice Marshal John Nmadu Yisa-Doko of Doko, the first airman to become Chief of the Air Staff.

## Lagos (Ikeja) (Lagos State)

Role: Logistics Command; 631 Aircraft Maintenance Depot.
Lagos was the first headquarters of the Nigerian Air Force, with the Ministry of Defence under Alhaji Muhammadu Ribadu serving as NAF Headquarters at the service's establishment in 1964.
On 13 August 1967 Biafran aircraft bombed Lagos, damaging the NAF fleet and installations. The Logistics Command was established in the city in 1982 and received its Colours from the President.
Lagos also carries the memory of the C-130H Hercules NAF 911, which crashed into the Ejigbo canal three minutes after take-off on 26 September 1992, killing all 158 on board.
Logistics Command, headquartered at Ikeja within Murtala Muhammed International Airport, supplies and maintains all NAF equipment through 631 Aircraft Maintenance Depot and the Equipment Supply Depot. Ikeja was long the home of the C-130 Hercules fleet of the Military Airlift Group.

## Enugu (Enugu State)

Role: Ground Training Command; 405 Helicopter Combat Training Group.
The first set of NAF instructor pilots trained at Enugu in the earliest years of the service. On 2 May 2018 the Ground Training Command was established in the city, having been unbundled from the former Training Command.
Enugu State is the home of Air Marshal Sunday Kelvin Aneke, the twenty-third and serving Chief of the Air Staff, after whom this Hall of Fame is named.
Enugu, within Akanu Ibiam International Airport, also hosts 405 Helicopter Combat Training Group and the Institute of Helicopter Flying Studies.

## Benin City (Edo State)

Role: 107 Air Maritime Group.
Benin City is the home of 107 Air Maritime Group of the Tactical Air Command; ATR-42 maritime patrol aircraft watch over the Gulf of Guinea from here.
Colonel Shittu Akanji Alao, the fourth Commander, died on 15 October 1969 when his aircraft crashed at Uzebba, about fifty miles north-west of Benin. He was 32 and alone in the aircraft, and was buried in Lagos with full military honours two days later.

## Yenagoa (Bayelsa State)

Role: Mobility Command.
On 29 March 2011 the Nigerian Air Force established the Mobility Command with its headquarters in Yenagoa, bringing airlift and helicopter mobility under one command in the Niger Delta.
The Mobility Command performs the airpower role of tactical and strategic airlift in support of military operations and government. Air Marshal Sunday Kelvin Aneke was its Air Officer Commanding until his appointment as Chief of the Air Staff in October 2025.

## Port Harcourt (Rivers State)

Role: 115 Special Operations Group.
Ilyushin Il-28 bombers delivered via Egypt during the civil war were based at Port Harcourt and Calabar from 1967. The Port Harcourt NAF Base at Rumuomasi is one of the four purely military airfields of the Air Force and hosts 115 Special Operations Group.
Rivers State is the home of Lieutenant Colonel George T. Kurubo of Bonny, who transferred from the Army in 1964 and in January 1966 became the first Nigerian to command the Air Force.

## Calabar (Cross River State)

Role: Air operations, South South.
Calabar hosted Il-28 bombers during the civil war and in December 1986 the Tactical Air Command established a unit of Aermacchi MB-339AN trainers in the city.
An air crash at Obudu, Cross River State, on 17 September 2006 claimed the lives of several Army officers.

## Jos (Plateau State)

Role: Air Force Military School; 563 NAF Hospital.
The Air Force Military School in Jos has educated and trained young airmen since 1980.
Plateau State is the home of Air Marshal Jonah Domfa Wuyep, the fourteenth Chief of the Air Staff.

## Bauchi (Bauchi State)

Role: Special Operations Command; 201 Composite Group.
On 19 November 2016 the Nigerian Air Force established the Special Operations Command in Bauchi, giving its Regiment and Special Forces a dedicated headquarters.
Bauchi State is the home of Air Marshal Sadique Baba Abubakar of Azare, the twentieth Chief of the Air Staff.
The Command was first announced by Air Marshal Sadique Abubakar in January 2016 and is responsible for base defence and force protection of NAF assets, with 201 Composite Group and the NAF Regiment Training Centre at Bauchi.

## Katsina (Katsina State)

Role: 410 Central Flying School.
Katsina hosts 410 Central Flying School of the Air Training Command and supports air operations in the North West, including interdiction missions along the Kaduna–Zamfara boundary. Katsina Air Force Base is listed as a dedicated military airfield.

## Sokoto (Sokoto State)

Role: 119 Composite Group.
Sokoto is a forward base for air operations in the North West, home to 119 Composite Group of the Tactical Air Command and the Senior Non-Commissioned Officers Leadership and Management Academy.

## Ilorin (Kwara State)

Role: Mobility Command airlift group.
Ilorin hosts an airlift group of the Mobility Command, supporting tactical and strategic airlift for military operations and government across the North Central region.

- Role: Basic trainer
- Notes: Fourteen trainers that taught the first generation of Nigerian pilots at Kaduna.

### Nord Noratlas
- Entered NAF service: 1965
- Origin: France
- Role: Transport
- Notes: The first airlift capability of the force.

### MiG-17F
- Entered NAF service: 1967
- Origin: Soviet Union
- Role: Fighter
- Notes: Delivered to Kano from Egypt during the civil war; the first combat aircraft of the NAF.

### Ilyushin Il-28
- Entered NAF service: 1967
- Origin: Soviet Union
- Role: Medium bomber
- Notes: Six bombers delivered from Egypt and stationed at Calabar and Port Harcourt.

### L-29 Delfin
- Entered NAF service: 1967
- Origin: Czechoslovakia
- Role: Jet trainer / light attack
- Notes: Retired and replaced by the L-39 Albatros in the 1980s.

### MiG-21MF / UM
- Entered NAF service: 1975
- Origin: Soviet Union
- Role: Interceptor
- Notes: Twenty-five MiG-21MF and six MiG-21UM delivered in 1975, later reinforced with 13 MiG-21bis and 2 UM. In storage from the 1990s; one is preserved as a gate guard in Abuja.

### C-130H Hercules
- Entered NAF service: 1975
- Origin: United States
- Role: Transport
- Notes: Peacekeeping, relief and ECOMOG airlift for four decades; operated by 88 Military Airlift Group, Ikeja.

### Alpha Jet
- Entered NAF service: 1981
- Origin: Germany / France
- Role: Light attack / trainer
- Notes: Twenty-four delivered; the workhorse of Nigerian air operations from Liberia to the North East, with additional airframes delivered and operationalised in 2025.

### SEPECAT Jaguar
- Entered NAF service: 1984
- Origin: UK / France
- Role: Strike fighter
- Notes: Eighteen delivered (13 Jaguar SN and 5 Jaguar BN), operated from Makurdi.

### Aermacchi MB-339AN
- Entered NAF service: 1986
- Origin: Italy
- Role: Advanced trainer
- Notes: Advanced jet training unit established at Calabar by Tactical Air Command.

### Aero L-39 Albatros
- Entered NAF service: 1980s
- Origin: Czechoslovakia
- Role: Armed jet trainer
- Notes: Twenty-four purchased to replace the L-29 Delfin.

### Chengdu F-7Ni / FT-7Ni
- Entered NAF service: 2009
- Origin: China
- Role: Fighter
- Notes: Twelve F-7Ni single-seaters and three FT-7Ni trainers, approved in 2005 for $251 million; the Nigerian variant carries western avionics.

### ATR-42 MP
- Entered NAF service: 2009
- Origin: Italy / France
- Role: Maritime patrol
- Notes: Two aircraft watching the Gulf of Guinea from 81 Air Maritime Group, Benin.

### Super Mushshak
- Entered NAF service: 2016
- Origin: Pakistan
- Role: Basic trainer
- Notes: Revived ab-initio flying training at Kaduna.

### Mi-35M
- Entered NAF service: 2017
- Origin: Russia
- Role: Attack helicopter
- Notes: Close air support in the North East; one was lost at Damasak in January 2019.

### Beechcraft King Air 360ER
- Entered NAF service: 2020
- Origin: United States
- Role: ISR
- Notes: Four intelligence, surveillance and reconnaissance aircraft; the first all-female ISR crew flew a King Air in May 2020.

### JF-17 Thunder
- Entered NAF service: 2021
- Origin: Pakistan / China
- Role: Multirole fighter
- Notes: Three Block II aircraft (NAF 720, 721, 722) inducted at the NAF Day celebration on 20 May 2021.

### A-29 Super Tucano
- Entered NAF service: 2021
- Origin: Brazil / United States
- Role: Light attack
- Notes: Twelve aircraft; the first six arrived in Kano on 22 July 2021 and were inducted in Abuja on 31 August 2021.

### M-346FA Master
- Entered NAF service: 2026
- Origin: Italy
- Role: Light combat / trainer
- Notes: Twenty-four on order; the first was accepted in July 2026.
