==============================================================================
1. DECADES
==============================================================================

1961 – 1971  Birth in a time of war
  From the lessons of the Congo to a battle-tested force in under a decade:
  German instructors, the first Nigerian pilots, and the crucible of the civil
  war.

1972 – 1982  Building the force
  A peacetime service takes shape. Hercules transports, MiG-21s and Alpha Jets
  arrive, and the institutions that still train officers today are founded.

1983 – 1993  Jaguars and expeditions
  Modern strike jets enter service, and in 1990 the Air Force deploys abroad
  for the first time with ECOMOG in Liberia.

1994 – 2004  Peacekeeping and renewal
  Operations over Sierra Leone, the return to democratic rule, and the long
  task of restoring a fleet worn down by sanctions.

2005 – 2015  Rebuilding the fleet
  New fighters from China, new partnerships, and the beginning of the
  counter-insurgency campaign in the North East.

2016 – 2026  A force reborn
  Super Tucanos, JF-17 Thunders, sixty years of service and a new generation
  of leaders.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1961
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

[1] 1961 — The idea of an air force is born
  The idea of establishing an air force for Nigeria was first mooted in 1961
  following the Nations participation in peace-keeping operations in Congo and
  Tanganyika (now Tanzania) where the Nigerian Army Regiment had no air
  transport of its own and were reliant on foreign air forces aircraft for
  airlift to and from the theatres of operation.
  Photo caption: Nigerian police unit in the Congo, 1961: the peacekeeping
  context
  Photos: assets/photos/06-timeline/1961-idea-born-congo-context.jpg

[27] 5 August 1967 — Colonel Shittu Akanji Alao appointed Fourth Commander
  Colonel Shittu Akanji Alao was appointed the Fourth Commander of the
  Nigerian Air Force. He held this office until his death on 15 October 1969
  while on a solo flight in an L-29 aircraft in the Southern-western part of
  the country when he ran into bad weather and subsequently ran out of fuel.
  He tried to make an emergency landing, but instead, ran into a tree and died
  in the process. He died at Uzebba, about 50 miles Northwest of Benin.
  He became the First Commander and Chief of the Air Staff to die on active
  duty.
  State: Edo

3. COMMANDERS AND CHIEFS OF THE AIR STAFF
In order of appointment. Numbers 1 and 2 were German Air Force commanders of
the NAF.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Colonel Gerhard Kahtz
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Short name: Gerhard Kahtz
  Rank: Colonel, German Air Force
  In office: 1963-05-05 to 1965-11-11
  Born: 1922
  Biography:
    A Luftwaffe officer, Kahtz led the German Air Force Technical Assistance
    Group (GAFTAG), which received the contract to establish the Nigerian Air
    Force. He became the first Commander of the NAF, serving from 5 May 1963
    to 11 November 1965.
  Notable in office:
    - Led GAFTAG, which built the NAF Tactical and Training Wing at Kaduna
    - First-generation aircraft: Dornier Do-27, Piaggio P.149D, Nord Noratlas
    and Alouette
    - Flying training began at Kaduna
  Sources: Wikipedia | Nigerian Air Force (airforce.mil.ng)
  Photo: assets/history/image10.jpeg

2. Colonel Wolfgang Thimmig
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Short name: Wolfgang Thimmig
  Rank: Colonel, German Air Force
  In office: 1965-11-24 to 1966-01-19
  Born: 1912
  Died: 1976
  Biography:
    Appointed the Second Commander of the Nigerian Air Force on 24 November
    1965, Thimmig held office for 56 days until GAFTAG handed over command to
    Nigerian officers on 19 January 1966.
  Notable in office:
    - Completed the handover of command to Nigerian officers (January 1966)
  Sources: Wikipedia
  Photo: assets/history/image33.jpeg

3. Lieutenant Colonel George Tamunoiyowuna Kurubo
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Short name: George T. Kurubo
  Rank: Lieutenant Colonel (later Brigadier)
  In office: 1966-01-19 to 1967-08-05
  Born: 27 July 1934, Bonny
  Died: 2000
  State of origin: Rivers
  Biography:
    Born in Bonny, Rivers State, Kurubo was commissioned into the Army in 1956
    and transferred to the newly formed Air Force as a Lieutenant Colonel in
    1964. On 19 January 1966 he became the Third Commander and the first
    Nigerian to lead the service. He left office in August 1967 on appointment
    as Ambassador to the Soviet Union.
  Notable in office:
    - First indigenous Commander of the Nigerian Air Force
    - First NAF hangar commissioned at Kaduna (January 1966)
    - Later Nigerian Ambassador to the Soviet Union
  Sources: Wikipedia | Nigerian Air Force (airforce.mil.ng)
  Photo: assets/history/image36.jpeg

4. Colonel Shittu Akanji Alao
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Short name: Shittu Akanji Alao
  Rank: Colonel
  In office: 1967-08-05 to 1969-10-15
  Born: 1937, Dorowa Babuje
  Died: 15 October 1969
  State of origin: Plateau
  Biography:
    Born in 1937 at Dorowa Babuje in present-day Plateau State, Alao was
    appointed the Fourth Commander on 5 August 1967 and led the Air Force
    through the most intense years of the civil war, acquiring MiG-17 fighters
    and Il-28 bombers. He died on 15 October 1969, aged 32, flying alone when
    his aircraft crashed at Uzebba, about fifty miles north-west of Benin. He
    was buried in Lagos with full military honours two days later.
  Notable in office:
    - Acquired the first combat aircraft: MiG-17F and Il-28
    - Primary Flying Training Wing established
    - First Commander to die on active duty

4. HISTORY OF THE COLLEGE
Armed Forces Command and Staff College, Jaji
==============================================================================

Established on 29 May 1976 with the help of the British Army Advisory Team,
the College at Jaji trains tactical and operational level officers of the
Army, Navy and Air Force in standardised command and staff duties.
  Hero photo: assets/college/college-hq.jpg
  Hero caption: Former College HQ Complex

  The Army Command and Staff College (ACSC) was established with assistance
  from the British Army Advisory Team (BAAT), led by Brigadier General T.A.
  Boam. General Olusegun Obasanjo, Head of State and Commander-in-Chief of the
  Armed Forces, delivered the inaugural speech. Brigadier General G.O. Ejiga
  was the pioneer Commandant.

6. BASES
Longitude/latitude place each base on the map of Nigeria.
==============================================================================

Abuja (FCT Abuja)
  Role: Headquarters Nigerian Air Force · Presidential Air Fleet
  Position: lon 7.49, lat 9.07
  Headquarters Nigerian Air Force sits in the Federal Capital Territory,
  having moved from Lagos after the seat of government relocated to Abuja.
  From here the Chief of the Air Staff directs the service and its Commands.
  Abuja is also the home of the Presidential Air Fleet. On 31 August 2021 the
  first six A-29 Super Tucano aircraft were formally inducted into the NAF
  Order of Battle at the 011 Presidential Air Fleet Apron, Nnamdi Azikiwe
  International Airport, with President Muhammadu Buhari presiding.
  The capital has seen the Air Force at its most visible: the Golden Jubilee
  celebrations of 2014, the induction of the CH-3A unmanned aircraft that same
  year, and the annual Independence Day flypasts rehearsed over the city.
  Photo: assets/history/image110.jpeg

Kaduna (Kaduna)
  Role: Air Training Command · 401 Flying Training School · AFIT
  Position: lon 7.44, lat 10.52
  Kaduna is where the Nigerian Air Force was born. Construction of the first
  NAF base began here in 1964, the German Air Force Technical Assistance Group
  built the Tactical and Training Wing, and the Light Liaison Transport
  Squadron (today 401 Flying Training School) was established to convert
  pilots trained overseas.
  The first NAF hangar was commissioned at Kaduna in January 1966, and in June
  1970 the first group of Nigerian-trained cadet pilots graduated here. Sir
  Ahmadu Bello and Prime Minister Tafawa Balewa visited the training centre in
  1965.
  Technical training grew from the Training Supply School of 1977 into the
  Technical Training Group, then 320 TTG, and in 2008 the Air Force Institute
  of Technology (AFIT), which designed the GULMA and TSAIGUMI unmanned
  aircraft. Kaduna remains the home of Air Training Command.
  Photo: assets/history/image23.jpeg

Jaji (Kaduna)
  Role: Armed Forces Command and Staff College
  Position: lon 7.42, lat 10.8
  Jaji, north of Kaduna, is home to the Armed Forces Command and Staff
  College, established in 1976 so that officers of the Army, Navy and Air
  Force could learn to plan and command together at the operational level.
  The Department of Air Warfare teaches airpower doctrine, air campaign
  planning and joint operations to every student of the College. The Air
  Marshal S.K. Aneke Hall of Fame stands here.
  Photo caption: The Armed Forces Command and Staff College campus, Jaji
  Photo: assets/photos/02-bases/jaji-college-campus.jpg

Makurdi (Benue)
  Role: Tactical Air Command · 101 Air Defence Group
  Position: lon 8.53, lat 7.73
  Makurdi became a key operating base during the civil war and has been the
  fighter heart of the Air Force ever since. Eighteen SEPECAT Jaguar strike
  fighters were delivered here in 1984, and in 1988 the Tactical Air Command
  was headquartered in the city.
  The Chengdu F-7Ni fighters arrived from 2009, and on 20 May 2021 three JF-17
  Thunder multirole fighters were inducted to mark the end of the NAF Day
  celebrations.
  Benue is also the home state of Brigadier Emmanuel E. Ikwue, the fifth
  Commander and the first to hold the office of Chief of the Air Staff.
  Headquarters Tactical Air Command directs all fighting units of the Air
  Force from Makurdi, alongside 101 Air Defence Group, 131 Engineering Group
  and the only NAF Central Armament Depot. The Air Force also operates its
  air-to-ground weapons range at Kwenev, near the city.
  Photo: assets/history/image69.jpeg

Kano (Kano)
  Role: 403 Flying Training School
  Position: lon 8.52, lat 12
  Kano received the first MiG-17F fighters delivered from Egypt during the
  civil war in 1967, the first combat aircraft of the Nigerian Air Force.
  On 22 July 2021 the first six A-29 Super Tucano aircraft landed at Kano
  after their ferry flight from the United States, received by the Minister of
  Defence, the Chief of Army Staff and the Chief of the Air Staff, Air Marshal
  Oladayo Amao.
  Kano State is the home of Air Marshal Hasan Bala Abubakar, the twenty-second
  Chief of the Air Staff.
  The first MiG-17s reached Kano via Egypt from 13 August 1967. Today Kano
  hosts 403 Flying Training School of the Air Training Command.
  Photo: assets/history/image143.jpeg

Maiduguri (Borno)
  Role: 105 Composite Group · North East operations
  Position: lon 13.15, lat 11.85
  Maiduguri is the forward hub of the air campaign in the North East. From
  here Alpha Jets, Mi-35M helicopters, Super Tucanos and ISR aircraft have
  supported ground troops in Operations Lafiya Dole and Hadin Kai.
  The campaign has cost lives: a Mi-35M attack helicopter was lost at Damasak
  on 2 January 2019 while providing close air support, and an Alpha Jet with
  Flight Lieutenant Chappelle and his crew went down near Borno on 31 March
  2021.
  Photo caption: Commissioning of an aircraft hangar in Maiduguri, 2020
  Photo: assets/photos/02-bases/maiduguri-hangar-opening-2020.jpg

Yola (Adamawa)
  Role: 103 Strike Group
  Position: lon 12.48, lat 9.2
  Yola supports air operations across the North East, with helicopters and
  light attack aircraft operating from the Adamawa capital.
  Adamawa is the home state of Air Marshal Alex Sabundu Badeh of Vimtim, the
  eighteenth Chief of the Air Staff, who became Chief of Defence Staff in
  January 2014.
  103 Strike Group of the Tactical Air Command operates from Yola.
  Photo caption: Personnel beside a NAF aircraft during a base visit, Yola,
  2024
  Photo: assets/photos/02-bases/yola-base-visit-2024.jpg

Kainji (Niger)
  Role: 407 Air Combat Training Group
  Position: lon 4.6, lat 9.87
  Kainji has hosted Alpha Jet operations since the 1980s and is now the home
  of the A-29 Super Tucano fleet inducted in 2021.
  Niger State is the home of Air Vice Marshal John Nmadu Yisa-Doko, one of the
  four pioneer pilots trained in Ethiopia, the first airman to become Chief of
  the Air Staff and the first to attain the rank of Air Vice Marshal.
  Kainji is one of only four purely military airfields of the NAF, with
  Kaduna, Makurdi and Port Harcourt, and hosts 407 Air Combat Training Group
  and 41 Force Protection Wing.
  Photo: assets/history/image147.jpeg

Minna (Niger)
  Role: NAF Detachment, Minna Airport
  Position: lon 6.55, lat 9.61
  The NAF Detachment at Minna operates from within Minna Airport, supporting
  air operations in the North Central region.
  Niger State is also the home of Kainji and of Air Vice Marshal John Nmadu
  Yisa-Doko of Doko, the first airman to become Chief of the Air Staff.
  Photo caption: Minna Airport runway, 2024
  Photo: assets/photos/02-bases/minna-airport-runway-2024.jpg

Lagos (Ikeja) (Lagos)
  Role: Logistics Command · 631 Aircraft Maintenance Depot
  Position: lon 3.33, lat 6.6
  Lagos was the first headquarters of the Nigerian Air Force, with the
  Ministry of Defence under Alhaji Muhammadu Ribadu serving as NAF
  Headquarters at the service's establishment in 1964.
  On 13 August 1967 Biafran aircraft bombed Lagos, damaging the NAF fleet and
  installations. The Logistics Command was established in the city in 1982 and
  received its Colours from the President.
  Lagos also carries the memory of the C-130H Hercules NAF 911, which crashed
  into the Ejigbo canal three minutes after take-off on 26 September 1992,
  killing all 158 on board.
  Logistics Command, headquartered at Ikeja within Murtala Muhammed
  International Airport, supplies and maintains all NAF equipment through 631
  Aircraft Maintenance Depot and the Equipment Supply Depot. Ikeja was long
  the home of the C-130 Hercules fleet of the Military Airlift Group.
  Photo: assets/history/image66.jpeg

Enugu (Enugu)
  Role: Ground Training Command · 405 Helicopter Combat Training Group
  Position: lon 7.5, lat 6.45
  The first set of NAF instructor pilots trained at Enugu in the earliest
  years of the service. On 2 May 2018 the Ground Training Command was
  established in the city, having been unbundled from the former Training
  Command.
  Enugu State is the home of Air Marshal Sunday Kelvin Aneke, the twenty-third
  and serving Chief of the Air Staff, after whom this Hall of Fame is named.
  Enugu, within Akanu Ibiam International Airport, also hosts 405 Helicopter
  Combat Training Group and the Institute of Helicopter Flying Studies.
  Photo caption: Akanu Ibiam International Airport, Enugu, 2021
  Photo: assets/photos/02-bases/enugu-airport-location-context-2021.jpg

Benin City (Edo)
  Role: 107 Air Maritime Group
  Position: lon 5.62, lat 6.33
  Benin City is the home of 107 Air Maritime Group of the Tactical Air
  Command; ATR-42 maritime patrol aircraft watch over the Gulf of Guinea from
  here.
  Colonel Shittu Akanji Alao, the fourth Commander, died on 15 October 1969
  when his aircraft crashed at Uzebba, about fifty miles north-west of Benin.
  He was 32 and alone in the aircraft, and was buried in Lagos with full
  military honours two days later.
  Photo caption: Inspection of 107 Air Maritime Group, Benin City, 2020
  Photo:
  assets/photos/02-bases/benin-city-107-air-maritime-group-visit-2020.jpg

Yenagoa (Bayelsa)
  Role: Mobility Command
  Position: lon 6.27, lat 4.92
  On 29 March 2011 the Nigerian Air Force established the Mobility Command
  with its headquarters in Yenagoa, bringing airlift and helicopter mobility
  under one command in the Niger Delta.
  The Mobility Command performs the airpower role of tactical and strategic
  airlift in support of military operations and government. Air Marshal Sunday
  Kelvin Aneke was its Air Officer Commanding until his appointment as Chief
  of the Air Staff in October 2025.
  Photo: assets/history/image102.jpeg

Port Harcourt (Rivers)
  Role: 115 Special Operations Group
  Position: lon 7, lat 4.82
  Ilyushin Il-28 bombers delivered via Egypt during the civil war were based
  at Port Harcourt and Calabar from 1967. The Port Harcourt NAF Base at
  Rumuomasi is one of the four purely military airfields of the Air Force and
  hosts 115 Special Operations Group.
  Rivers State is the home of Lieutenant Colonel George T. Kurubo of Bonny,
  who transferred from the Army in 1964 and in January 1966 became the first
  Nigerian to command the Air Force.
  Photo: assets/history/image36.jpeg

Calabar (Cross River)
  Role: Air operations, South South
  Position: lon 8.33, lat 4.95
  Calabar hosted Il-28 bombers during the civil war and in December 1986 the
  Tactical Air Command established a unit of Aermacchi MB-339AN trainers in
  the city.
  An air crash at Obudu, Cross River State, on 17 September 2006 claimed the
  lives of several Army officers.
  Photo: assets/history/image72.jpeg

Jos (Plateau)
  Role: Air Force Military School · 563 NAF Hospital
  Position: lon 8.89, lat 9.92
  The Air Force Military School in Jos has educated and trained young airmen
  since 1980.
  Plateau State is the home of Air Marshal Jonah Domfa Wuyep, the fourteenth
  Chief of the Air Staff.
  Photo caption: Commissioning of military-school projects, Jos, 2020
  Photo: assets/photos/02-bases/jos-military-schools-ceremony-2020.jpg

Bauchi (Bauchi)
  Role: Special Operations Command · 201 Composite Group
  Position: lon 9.84, lat 10.31
  On 19 November 2016 the Nigerian Air Force established the Special
  Operations Command in Bauchi, giving its Regiment and Special Forces a
  dedicated headquarters.
  Bauchi State is the home of Air Marshal Sadique Baba Abubakar of Azare, the
  twentieth Chief of the Air Staff.
  The Command was first announced by Air Marshal Sadique Abubakar in January
  2016 and is responsible for base defence and force protection of NAF assets,
  with 201 Composite Group and the NAF Regiment Training Centre at Bauchi.
  Photo caption: Regiment training at the Bauchi obstacle course, 2020
  Photo: assets/photos/02-bases/bauchi-regiment-training-2020.jpg

Katsina (Katsina)
  Role: 410 Central Flying School
  Position: lon 7.6, lat 12.99
  Katsina hosts 410 Central Flying School of the Air Training Command and
  supports air operations in the North West, including interdiction missions
  along the Kaduna–Zamfara boundary. Katsina Air Force Base is listed as a
  dedicated military airfield.
  Photo caption: Officials beside a trainer during the Katsina airport project
  visit, 2020
  Photo: assets/photos/02-bases/katsina-air-operations-project-visit-2020.jpg

Sokoto (Sokoto)
  Role: 119 Composite Group
  Position: lon 5.24, lat 13.06
  Sokoto is a forward base for air operations in the North West, home to 119
  Composite Group of the Tactical Air Command and the Senior Non-Commissioned
  Officers Leadership and Management Academy.
  Photo caption: The Chief of the Air Staff at Sokoto Government House during
  his 119 Composite Group tour, 2024
  Photo:
  assets/photos/02-bases/sokoto-government-house-security-visit-2024.jpg

Ilorin (Kwara)
  Role: Mobility Command airlift group
  Position: lon 4.55, lat 8.49
  Ilorin hosts an airlift group of the Mobility Command, supporting tactical
  and strategic airlift for military operations and government across the
  North Central region.
  Photo caption: Ilorin International Airport, 2022
  Photo:
  assets/photos/02-bases/ilorin-civilian-airport-location-context-2022.jpg
